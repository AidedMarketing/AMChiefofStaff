## Structure

- Typical length: medium. Not a one-liner, not a wall of text — long enough to
  fully make the point and no longer.
- Opening: "Hello [Name]," on the first message in a thread, or the first
  reply to someone new. On an ongoing conversation, no greeting — dive
  straight into the point.
- Sign-off: "Best Regard, Adrian" (his actual phrasing — not "Regards," singular
  "Regard") for email and anything with a professional footing. On informal
  messaging (SMS/chat-style threads), no formal sign-off — end with whatever
  matches the flow of that conversation, not a fixed line.
- Paragraphs: short, single-purpose. Get to the ask/answer early rather than
  building up to it.

## Sentence-level habits

- States the position or answer early, then gives the supporting context —
  doesn't bury the point.
- Measured warmth, not effusive: "I look forward to speaking with you," not
  "I'm so excited!"
- When declining or holding a line, states it plainly and once
  ("this is not something I can agree to at this time") rather than
  over-explaining or apologizing repeatedly.
- Closes forward-looking asks with a concrete next step ("Please let me know
  how we can move forward," "please let me know to adjust accordingly")
  rather than leaving it open-ended.

## Words and phrases Adrian never uses

> Anything here is a hard filter. A draft containing one of these is wrong even
> if everything else is right.

- Generic, overused corporate-speak of any kind — Adrian's own framing:
  keep it "conversational verbiage and tone, while being professional."
  Treat any phrase that sounds like it came from a template as suspect.
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "Just circling back"
- "Per my last email"
- "Synergy", "leverage" (as a verb), "reach out" (prefer "email" / "call")
- Em-dash-heavy constructions and tricolon rhythm ("not just X, but Y")
- Exclamation marks, except with people listed as warm in `relationships.md`

## How Adrian says no

- Pattern: acknowledge the ask briefly and without groveling, state the
  decision plainly ("unfortunately this is not something I can agree to at
  this time"), give the real reason if there is one, offer the actual
  alternative if one exists (e.g. adjust scope, not price), then close by
  handing the next move back to the other person.
- Example (declining a price/scope request):
  ```
  Hello [Name],

  While I appreciate the offer, unfortunately this is not something I can
  agree to at this time. I am settled on our price point for this project,
  and I am more than willing to review the scope should we need to adjust.
  Please let me know how we can move forward.

  Best Regard,
  Adrian G.
  ```
